# F6 — Part II needs a failure beat

Part I earns each historical transition by showing what the current frame
cannot hold. Part II currently moves only upward: conversation simplifies the
interface, the tube reveals its structure, apparatus returns, and projections
promise flexible views. Without a cost, that sequence reads as a concept video
rather than an argument.

The correction belongs in Slice C (`cost`), after projections, not in the
prerequisite fixes or Recovery:

- a projection changes without the reader initiating the change;
- one operation the reader was tracking is visibly displaced;
- the composition briefly loses a stable shared reference;
- body copy names the resulting problems: unstable mental model, weak
  cross-session citation, loss of shared pointing, and vendor-selected framing;
- the disturbance remains restrained and resolves to a stable review state.

The visual claim is not that dynamic framing fails. It is that adaptability has
an epistemic and social cost: when the frame moves, certainty about where a
thing is—and whether another person sees the same thing—moves with it.

Implementation constraints remain binding: reuse the durable operation IDs,
move them only through the shared positional writer, do not destroy or duplicate
tracked operations, and give reduced motion a meaningful post-displacement
plateau rather than replacing the beat with zero-duration animation.
