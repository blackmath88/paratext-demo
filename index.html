A History of Framing

How text learns to
wear interfaces

Text did not become an interface. It repeatedly acquired frames, annotations, navigation and apparatus — and each layer was added by readers before it was standardized by machines.

Scroll

The acts

01

Bare text

Text begins as a stream.

A page. Just the words. No frame, no guide, no context. Continuous script with barely a mark to tell a reader where anything begins or ends.

02

Glosses

Readers speak back.

Voices arrive in the margins. Underlines, reference marks, interlinear corrections, a hand pointing at a line worth keeping. The text has not changed. It has acquired readers, and their apparatus accumulates around it.

03

Print

Apparatus becomes standard.

What one reader improvised, the press makes systematic. The rough underline straightens into a typographic rule. The marginal citation descends into a footnote. Spacing regularizes, a title sets, a page number appears. The page becomes a thing with parts.

04 — 08

Editorial · Terminal · GUI · Overflow · Reframing

The remaining acts follow the same object into columns, into the machine, into the browser, into the flood of generated language, and out the other side into a frame that reorganizes around the work. They are not built yet.
